# Run-sheet output pattern

## Before the plan

For an incomplete planning brief, give confirmed facts and consequential questions or proposed assumptions, then wait. Do not append a detailed agenda. State minor working assumptions separately. For a complete or explicitly authorized provisional brief, proceed without a redundant confirmation step.

## Confirmed brief and selection

State the phase/session endpoint, tangible deliverable, available evidence, participants and relevant experience, format, time units and availability, access to participants/data, tools/materials, decision owner, and accepted assumptions. Identify the chosen source scope and any relevant access limitations. Do not present inferred facts as user-confirmed.

Show a concise comparison of the strongest relevant methods, with prerequisites, decisive tradeoffs and source entries actually checked. Explain the recommendation and sequence. Do not reproduce the full catalogue or claim full-book verification from a few passages.

## Agenda

Use a table with start, end, minutes, stage/activity, intended output, and facilitator cue. Include opening, each activity, transitions, breaks, buffer, and closure. State the checked total and hard-stop time.

For a multi-week project phase, use period, activity, owner/staffing, active effort and its units, elapsed dependencies, and output/decision. Respect uneven availability. Separate team meetings from parallel individual work and waiting for recruitment/responses. Do not force a session rhythm onto a whole week. Provide detailed session agendas only where useful or requested.

## Activity cards

For each selected activity, include:

- Why it fits, book/course source, exact method name, and source section/step headings; no displayed page numbers. Do not substitute generic or original exercises for verified methods.
- Input, output, materials, grouping, and capture location.
- A short launch prompt and clear participant steps.
- Component timing that adds to the agenda allocation, including reporting and debrief.
- The completion criterion and how the output feeds the next stage.
- Source prescription and any proposed change, confirmed reason, retained mechanism, and limitation, beside the affected activity. Identify whether a consequential adaptation is approved or still awaiting approval; do not schedule an unapproved core change as settled.
- Likely friction and a practical fallback. When tighter constraints change feasibility, compare fitting alternatives before revising the agenda.

## Delivery notes

Include preparation and room/board setup that must happen before the start. Give useful time checks and a concrete overrun cut. Explain how unused buffer will support review or early closure without changing the published hard stop. End with how decisions, owners, and follow-up are captured.

Scale detail to the task. For a brief planning request, provide the agenda and essential selection rationale. For a ready-to-lead session, provide the full run sheet and activity cards.

For generated participant materials, identify the exact method instructions/cautions applied, distinguish generated wording from book text, and include relevant piloting and interpretation limits. Keep this note concise; perform the quality check even when the user asks for a short answer.

Before delivery, check that each method receives its prerequisites, the sequence feeds the confirmed endpoint, and budgets include all necessary work. Report changed assumptions or constraints and their consequences when revising an earlier plan.

For documentation, include the planning approach/model and a step-level traceability table for every creative or evaluative stage. Mixed methods need separate attribution per borrowed step, including what changed. Use step numbers or headings, not page numbers; retain page locations internally for checking.
