# Realistic timing and recovery

These are facilitator planning defaults, not claims from either book. Replace them when the brief, source guidance, or actual conditions justify different estimates.

## Build the time budget

Confirm ambiguous time units before budgeting. Shared elapsed time, hours per person and total team person-hours are different constraints. Four people working together for 30 minutes consume two person-hours. Confirm whether the budget covers preparation, recruitment, fieldwork, analysis and documentation, or only scheduled sessions. Do not multiply ambiguous hours by team size without confirmation.

Compute the available minutes from start and end, or the confirmed total duration. Deduct fixed opening, breaks, closure, and contingency before allocating session activity time. Include any lunch or required interruption. Ask for a timezone only when coordinating participants across locations or dates.

For project phases, respect availability by week/day and roles. Count parallel work once per person's labour; distinguish active labour from elapsed waiting for recruitment, responses, homework or fabrication. State dependencies and uncertainty. Do not assume that a small labour allowance guarantees recruitment within the calendar window. Confirm access or offer a conditional option before committing to a dependent method. Unknown team experience can change preparation, facilitation and analysis effort substantially.

For each activity, budget explanation and demonstration, setup/grouping, individual work, group work, reporting, and synthesis/debrief. Internal components must sum to the activity row; do not count them twice. Give transitions separate rows for moving people, changing materials, opening boards, and carrying outputs forward.

Working estimates to adjust:

- A simple instruction: 2–4 minutes; unfamiliar multistep activity: 5–8 minutes including an example and understanding check.
- A simple transition: 2–3 minutes; movement, regrouping, or board changes: 4–7 minutes.
- Reporting: number of speakers × speaking allowance, plus handoffs and synthesis. Eight tables at 2 minutes each require at least 16 minutes before handoffs or synthesis.
- For sustained sessions, normally provide a 10–15 minute break after roughly 60–90 minutes of demanding work. Adjust for accessibility, energy, format, and the user's constraints. A brief reset may suffice in a short session; do not add a long break mechanically.
- Reserve about 5–10% of the total window as explicit contingency, more when conditions are uncertain. If the window is very short, use a small stated buffer and fewer activities.
- Protect enough closing time to capture decisions, owners, and next steps; commonly 5–10 minutes, longer for complex commitments.

## Arithmetic check

Within a session, use one time system consistently, preferably start/end clock times plus duration. Every row starts when the previous row ends. Sum durations and verify the final end matches the hard stop. Breaks, transitions, and buffer are part of the total. For each activity, also check its component budget. Report the total and whether all rows fit.

For project plans, check both calendar dependencies and the confirmed labour/availability limits. Label the units in tables; do not add elapsed waits to person-hours or double-count individual work already included in a group block. Recompute every affected total after a correction to hours, staffing or availability.

Use realistic numbers rather than implying that estimates guarantee completion. Name assumptions that affect timing, especially participant count, number of reports, and complexity of the task. A schedule needing more time than the window is infeasible even if its individual activities sound reasonable.

## Recovery during delivery

At checkpoints, compare actual progress with the run sheet. Spend the stated buffer first when that is appropriate. Then shorten optional reporting, reduce the number of ideas developed, or remove a lower-priority activity. Keep essential synthesis, decisions, breaks, and closure where possible. State the lost scope or output and issue a revised remaining schedule. Recheck prerequisites after moving work: only bring analysis forward if its source records already exist. When fieldwork is delayed, identify independent work or unspent time instead of swapping in analysis that depends on the delayed fieldwork.

A material change in budget, participant access or team capacity also triggers the method comparison in [activity-selection.md](activity-selection.md). Consider feasible replacements proactively rather than only shortening the same method. Explain effects on evidence depth and deliverables. Obtain agreement before relying on an unapproved core adaptation or consequential scope reduction.

Do not recover an overrun by accelerating unfamiliar instructions or assuming everyone will work faster. If the core deliverable no longer fits, offer an achievable reduced deliverable and identify what needs follow-up.
