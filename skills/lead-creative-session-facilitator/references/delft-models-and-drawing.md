## Design Drawing as a Language

Source text: [photo](books/delft-supplement/design-drawing-as-a-language.jpg), printed 39. Supporting image: [cave paintings and drawing illustration](books/delft-supplement/drawing-language-illustration.jpg), preceding unnumbered page inferred as 38 from the spread.

- Source category: Perspective, not a fixed numbered workshop exercise.
- Fit: visual exploration and communication throughout designing; supports developing thoughts, exchanging ideas, and discussing proposals with others.
- Source headings: What & Why; Mindset; How; Tips & Concerns; Limitations. The source gives no numbered procedure, fixed duration, or participant count.
- Core guidance: use drawing to externalize observations and concepts; tailor the representation to purpose, audience, decision, information flow, and design stage. The How section relates drawing to Discover, Define, Develop, and Deliver and points to the stage-specific drawing methods elsewhere in the book.
- Constraints: drawing takes practice. Begin with basic shapes and gradually increase complexity; choose clear composition, viewpoints, lines, and tonal values. Later work may require other formats, often preceded by sketches.
- Application (facilitator adaptation): when drawing becomes an actual activity, select and read the relevant named stage-specific drawing method. Reference this perspective for the rationale and that method for its steps; do not invent numbered steps and attribute them to this perspective.

## Reasoning in Design

Source text: [photo](books/delft-supplement/reasoning-in-design.jpg), printed 43. Supporting model: [diagram](books/delft-supplement/reasoning-in-design-diagram.jpg), preceding unnumbered page inferred as 42.

- Source category: Model of design reasoning, not a session timetable.
- Fit: structuring insights, generating questions, communicating reasoning, and reflecting on how a proposed form addresses needs and values. Primarily product-oriented but applicable to services.
- Source How headings: Form; Properties; Function; Needs and Values. These are components, not numbered stages.
- Analysis: reason from form, properties, and usage context toward predicted functioning and consequences.
- Synthesis: reason from user values and needs toward functions, properties, and a suitable form. The diagram identifies these as deductive analysis and abductive synthesis.
- Output: explicit relationships connecting proposed form and material, properties, functions, needs, and values, with mode and conditions of use considered.
- Constraints: consider usage context as carefully as the product. Properties can have desirable and undesirable consequences; intended functions can differ from uses people discover. Intuition and creativity remain necessary.
- Application (facilitator adaptation): a mapping exercise must cite the selected How components and the analysis/synthesis direction used. Its prompts, grouping, and time allocation are adaptations, not source-prescribed instructions.

## Basic Design Cycle

Source text: [photo](books/delft-supplement/basic-design-cycle.jpg), printed 45. Supporting model: [diagram](books/delft-supplement/basic-design-cycle-diagram.jpg), preceding unnumbered page inferred as 44.

- Source category: Model of iterative reasoning. Its five reasoning steps are not five project phases and must not be presented as a one-pass project timeline.
- Fit: examining whether a design process has skipped necessary reasoning; organizing thought, action, evaluation, and decisions at different levels of detail.
- Source How headings and corresponding diagram outputs:
  1. Analyse: examine the goal/problem and relevant aspects, informing criteria and requirements. Diagram output: Criteria.
  2. Synthesis: generate possible solutions or elements of proposals. Diagram output: Provisional Design.
  3. Simulate: create imagined, digital, or physical representations of proposals so their consequences can be considered. Diagram output: Expected Properties.
  4. Evaluate: assess potential value through those representations against design criteria. Diagram output: Value of the Design.
  5. Decide: judge relative value and choose whether to proceed, repeat, or focus on other elements. Diagram output: Approved Design, with iteration possible.
- The numbered mapping above corresponds to the diagram; the text itself uses heading labels. Use the exact heading as the reference when documenting a borrowed reasoning step.
- Constraints: a cycle may occur within seconds or over weeks. Entry can be at different points, but complete the reasoning cycle. Reflect and discuss missing steps rather than jumping directly to solutions.
- Application (facilitator adaptation): use this model to check a proposed session sequence. A session may only advance part of a larger cycle; identify what was completed and what must happen after the session. Do not claim a short session produces an approved design simply because it includes Decide.
