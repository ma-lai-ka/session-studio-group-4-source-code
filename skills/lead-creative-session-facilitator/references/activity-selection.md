# Activity selection

## Outcome first

Start from the confirmed endpoint and evidence. If either is consequentially unclear, use the brief checkpoint in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) before selecting a sequence. Identify only the work needed to reach that endpoint. A useful sequence may span framing, divergence, development and convergence, but do not force every stage into every session or add a concept deliverable to a problem-definition phase.

## Screen, inspect, compare, then select

1. Screen the entire [source catalogue](catalogue.md) within the requested source scope and the whole [Delft selection index](delft-selection-index.md) for Delft work. If a file read is truncated, retrieve the omitted ranges before claiming complete screening. Consider the actual decision and evidence needs across the catalogue's categories; phase labels are navigation, not hard eligibility rules. Include plausible candidates outside the eight starter summaries. Do not add irrelevant methods simply to demonstrate breadth.
2. Build a candidate set by purpose, required inputs, output, constraints and source availability. Familiarity or an existing summary is not a reason to stop searching. Treat index descriptions as editorial screening aids; unresolved entries need source investigation before use.
3. Inspect actual passages for plausible shortlisted candidates, including purpose, How, Tips & Concerns, limitations and relevant figures. Inspect a competing candidate before rejecting it on a source-specific claim. Do not infer procedures from a title, generic model, website tag, or neighbouring method. Disclose inaccessible passages and keep their procedures unverified.
4. Compare candidates on purpose, evidence/prerequisites, usable output, participant experience, group composition, tools, accessibility, and complete delivery effort. Include recruitment, preparation, analysis and elapsed waiting, not only the visible exercise. Assess whether the available evidence can support the desired conclusion.
5. Show a concise shortlist: **candidate | fit and prerequisites | decisive constraint/tradeoff | source checked or gap**. Recommend the strongest fit and explain why plausible alternatives were not selected. The number of candidates follows the problem; do not force a fixed count or display the entire catalogue. Keep a fuller comparison available if requested.
6. Only then assemble the sequence. If no verified method fits, explain what must change or what remains unresolved. Do not substitute an invented workshop exercise for a missing source procedure.

Checking selected methods' descriptions is not evidence that they were the best choices. Check both source fidelity and suitability for the confirmed brief. Report the scope honestly: the catalogue was screened and named passages inspected, not the entire book reread.

## Connect the sequence

For each stage, name the input and output and verify the next stage can consume it. If a prerequisite is missing, obtain it, schedule a justified source method to produce it, or select a different method. A later stage cannot retrospectively supply an earlier prerequisite. Reserve time to consolidate raw material before selecting. Agree selection criteria before voting or evaluation. Use the confirmed decision owner/rule; do not silently impose majority voting.

Keep research findings separate from interpretations, hypotheses, problem framings and concepts. Multiple findings do not automatically become mutually exclusive directions. Do not invent a causal ordering to make the plan coherent. Investigate what the evidence supports and what the group disagrees about. For example, a concept-comparison method requiring comparable concepts and prioritized requirements cannot be treated as a default way to rank raw research themes.

Process models can explain or check reasoning. When scheduling substantive research, synthesis or design work, give an executable source-grounded procedure rather than using “Analyse” or “Simulate” as the entire instruction. Preserve iteration where warranted; do not claim every method has a universal fixed order.

Adapt reporting to scale. Calculate serial sharing explicitly; use pairs, table representatives, a gallery, or written clustering when a full round would consume the session. Account for the synthesis work those alternatives still require.

Use quiet individual work when it improves participation before group discussion. Give accessible alternatives to movement, rapid drawing, or speaking on demand when relevant. Make remote instructions and capture locations explicit. Treat energy changes as design choices, not as reasons to add unrelated games.

## Compare before adapting

Check whether another source method fits the constraints without changing its core mechanism. Compare that option with an adaptation; do not stretch a familiar method merely to keep the original agenda.

For each proposed adaptation, record beside the activity:

| Source prescription | Proposed change | Confirmed reason | Mechanism retained | Effect or uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exact method and source step/heading, briefly paraphrased | Retained, adapted, reordered or omitted elements | User constraint or observed condition | Why the method can still serve the intended purpose | Lost depth, changed output, evidence limit or untested estimate |

Distinguish a source-permitted option from an adaptation: if the book explicitly permits summarising notes, selecting that option is not itself a changed procedure. A new one-page template or a fixed analysis cutoff remains facilitator judgment.

If core steps, essential inputs or intended outputs change, explain the alternative and seek the designer's approval before relying on that change in a detailed plan. Explicit authorization of that particular change already counts; do not ask again. Routine materials, roles and timing estimates can proceed with labels when they preserve the method. If the core mechanism cannot survive the constraint, recommend another method or reduced scope instead of describing the result as equivalent to the original.

For a hybrid, attribute each substantive step to its own source. A blanket citation or “adapted” label does not justify the combination.

## Reconsider feasibility when constraints change

Reopen the affected comparison when time, access, evidence, skills or tools change. Reuse passages already checked when still applicable; inspect newly relevant candidates. Compare retaining the method, adapting it, replacing it and reducing the deliverable. Present feasible alternatives proactively with their access requirements, effort and evidence tradeoffs. Do not claim a questionnaire eliminates recruitment or that observation establishes participants' unreported feelings.

A candidate's quoted activity duration may exclude explanation, grouping, sharing or debrief. Estimate its full footprint before selecting it. Recruitment/distribution access, response delays, preparation and analysis require explicit allowances or acknowledged uncertainty. Where those dependencies determine feasibility, ask rather than invent a guaranteed turnaround. Use [timing-and-recovery.md](timing-and-recovery.md) for elapsed time and labour budgets.

Keep a fallback only for plausible friction: unclear prompt, low idea volume, disagreement, technology failure, or overrun. Specify the trigger and response. Fallbacks should preserve the objective while simplifying mechanics.

## Check generated materials

Read the relevant complete method entry before drafting participant materials. Extract the source's requirements for preparation, question/criterion construction, ordering, execution, review/piloting and interpretation. Check the generated material against those requirements before presenting it. Do not mistake a summary of How for coverage of Tips & Concerns.

For questionnaires and interview guides, check each question's purpose, unsupported premises, clarity, respondent burden and relevance to the evidence needed. Allow positive, negative and absent experiences where the brief leaves them open; do not assume all respondents share the problem or have attended an activity. Check answer options and branching against the question; do not turn qualitative guidance into an unsupported claim of statistical validity. Include the source's piloting/preparation steps when applicable.

These review checks are facilitator quality controls unless an exact source passage supports attribution to DDG. Project-specific wording and templates are generated drafts, not book-supplied or validated instruments. Briefly state the source basis and any departures with the material. Recheck source facts under criticism; change weak applications without discarding a verified source claim.
